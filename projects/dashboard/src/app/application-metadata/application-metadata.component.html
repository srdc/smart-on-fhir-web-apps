AUC Estimate and Its Uncertainty
AUC measures how well the model distinguishes between patients who develop the condition and those who do not.
AUC: {{ selectedDisease.discrimination?.auc | number:'1.3-3' }}
0 Chance: 0.5 1
Circle: estimated AUC ({{ selectedDisease.discrimination?.auc | number:'1.3-3' }})
Horizontal line: 95% confidence interval for this AUC estimate ({{ selectedDisease.discrimination?.ci_95_lo | number:'1.3-3' }} – {{ selectedDisease.discrimination?.ci_95_hi | number:'1.3-3' }})
Performance: An AUC closer to 1 indicates better discrimination. An AUC of 0.5 corresponds to chance-level discrimination.
Uncertainty: The 95% confidence interval shows the uncertainty around this AUC estimate. A narrower interval indicates a more precise AUC estimate, while a wider interval indicates greater uncertainty. A narrow interval does not necessarily indicate good performance; the AUC value and the interval must be interpreted together.
Calibration Slope
Compares how strongly the model separates lower-risk and higher-risk predictions. The ideal reference value is 1.
Reported slope: {{ selectedDisease.calibration?.calibration_slope | number:'1.3-3' }}
Below 1
Risk differences may be exaggerated
Higher-risk predictions may be too high, while lower-risk predictions may be too low.
Ideal: 1
Risk differences are appropriately scaled
The separation between lower-risk and higher-risk predictions is consistent with the observed outcomes.
Above 1
Risk differences may be compressed
Higher-risk predictions may be too low, while lower-risk predictions may be too high.
Brier Score
Measures the mean squared difference between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes.
{{ selectedDisease.calibration?.brier_score | number:'1.3-3' }}
Lower values indicate smaller prediction errors, and 0 represents perfect probabilistic predictions. The score should be compared with disease prevalence and an appropriate reference model rather than interpreted using a universal threshold.
Calibration Method

Calibration adjusts predicted probabilities so that they more closely correspond to observed outcome frequencies.
